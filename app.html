2. Sugar to add (chapitalization)

1 Brix is 10 g of sugar per litre of must (definition, ideal volume - adding sugar also raises volume a little). Published conversion: potential ABV = Brix x 0.55. In cool climates and for legal limits, check your local rules before chaptalizing.

3. Acid correction

Published rules of thumb: 1 g/L tartaric acid raises TA about 1 g/L; 1 g/L calcium carbonate lowers it about 1 g/L; potassium bicarbonate about 0.6 g/L per g/L. Commonly published targets: reds 6-8 g/L, whites 6.5-8.5 g/L (as tartaric). Bench-test first: pH shift does not track TA one-for-one.

5. Fortify (Pearson's square)

Pearson's square (published blending rule): parts spirit per parts wine = (target - wine) / (spirit - target). Distillation of alcohol is licensed in many places - use legally purchased spirit.

6. Ferment result, blend and dilute

Published homebrew conversion: ABV = (OG - FG) x 131.25. Dilution math is ideal - temperature contraction shrinks real mixtures slightly.
